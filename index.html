Clinic

LLM-based clinical AI agents for EHR phenotyping

Prof. Hua Xu Yale University · Research Assistant

Phenotyping algorithms decide which patients in a health record "have" a disease, and today they are hand-written as long, site-specific SQL. I study whether an LLM agent can read a published phenotype definition and produce SQL that actually runs, and finds the right patients, on Yale New Haven Health's OMOP data.

3 phenotypes: T2DM, dementia, hypothyroidism 80.0% F1 on T2DM GPT-5.4 agent pipelines

Pipeline

1
Phenotype definitionseMERGE algorithms from PheKB and PheMap
2
Gold-standard labelsRule-based SQL in DuckDB on YNHH OMOP CDM
3
LLM agentGPT-5.4 with alpha, beta and standard prompts, tool calling, skills
4
Generated SQLPhenotyping query for each disease
5
Execute and debugMake queries runnable; log manual edits
6
EvaluateRecall, F1, and two PPV settings

Question

Which combination of prompting strategy, tool access, and disease-specific versus generic skills gets an LLM agent closest to expert phenotyping logic?

What I did

  • Implemented eMERGE algorithms for three diseases and derived gold labels with DuckDB and SQL.
  • Built agent pipelines comparing prompt styles, tool calling, and skill files.
  • Debugged generated SQL on OMOP and catalogued common manual edits and site-specific warehouse mismatches.
  • Designed labeled-only and unlabeled-inclusive PPV, so over-prediction becomes measurable.

What we found

Agents reached strong agreement on T2DM and dementia. Hypothyroidism lagged, and error analysis traced it to missing race-specific criteria and over-inclusion of diagnosis codes.

Synthetic cohort generation and robustness studies for AI-based clinical trial matching

Prof. Tina Hernandez-Boussard Stanford University · Research Assistant (remote) · Mentor: Soumyadeep Roy

Testing AI trial-matching tools needs patients who meet, or almost meet, a trial's eligibility rules, and real records with those exact profiles are scarce and sensitive. I built a pipeline that turns a trial's eligibility criteria into a working Synthea module, generates a synthetic cohort, then stresses TrialGPT with controlled perturbations.

Question

Can we generate realistic cohorts on demand for any trial, and does an LLM trial matcher stay consistent when patient information is incomplete or noisy?

  • Connected the cohort to TrialGPT and wrote the perturbation and evaluation tooling.

~200 tasks in the benchmark Nature under revision

Tasks I designed

Toxicophore detectionhERG predictionPAINS filtering and classificationMetabolic soft spots"Cyanide trap" detection

Stress tests: corrupted datasets and conflicting instructions.

DrugPlayGround: benchmarking LLMs and embeddings for drug discovery

Prof. Hongyu Zhao Yale University · Research Assistant · Co-first author

Most drug-discovery models represent a molecule by its structure. We asked whether what language models know about a drug, its mechanism, uses and interactions, carries pharmacological signal that structure alone misses, and built a unified benchmark to test it.

+54% drug synergy R² +22% drug–protein interaction accuracy 100k+ generated drug descriptions

Benchmark pipeline

1
Drug datasets4 sets, 1,000+ cancer drugs each, PubChem and Hugging Face
2
PromptingNormal, CoT and Meta prompts
3
5 LLMsGPT, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek, Mistral on Yale HPC
4
5 embedding modelsText to drug representations
5
Downstream tasksSynergy, drug–protein interaction, perturbation, ADMET
6
Versus baselinesUni-Mol, ChemCPA, RDKit

Question

Do LLM-derived drug embeddings capture pharmacology beyond molecular structure, and how stable are the descriptions themselves?

What I did

  • Curated the four drug datasets.
  • Engineered the three prompting strategies and ran batch inference with Slurm.
  • Implemented text-quality evaluation and temperature-sensitivity analysis.
  • Produced the comparative visualizations.

What we found

LLM-derived embeddings consistently beat state-of-the-art molecular foundation models.

Diabetes prevention chatbots for dietary self-management in the Chinese population

Prof. Ming-Chun Huang Duke Kunshan University · Research Investigator · First author

Most diet advice in health chatbots is built around Western guidelines and food. I curated China-specific diabetes prevention evidence, fine-tuned GPT models on it, and shipped a web app that gives culturally grounded dietary and lifestyle guidance.

IEEE EMBC 2025 first author 80+ curated dialogues Deployed Streamlit app

Pipeline

1
Evidence synthesis5+ papers into a China-specific guideline
2
Dialogue dataset80+ diet and lifestyle conversations
3
Fine-tuningGPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4o via OpenAI API
4
EvaluationLoss curves plus scenario-based review
5
Web appStreamlit, Firebase login, chat history, export

Question

Can a lightly fine-tuned LLM give evidence-based, culturally appropriate prevention advice to Chinese-speaking users?

What I did

  • Led the project end to end as first author.
  • Built the guideline and dataset, ran fine-tuning, and designed the evaluation.
  • Developed and deployed the app.

What we found

Fine-tuned GPT-3.5-turbo generalized better than GPT-4o with lower validation loss, so it powers the deployed app.
